Every note on this harmonica

Greyed cells are duplicates — the same pitch is available without the slide, so take that one.

Where the pattern comes from

Solo tuning repeats every four holes: blow gives C E G C, draw gives D F A B. Pressing the slide raises whatever you're playing by one semitone, which fills in every black key.

Why some notes have two fingerings

C appears as blow 4 and blow 5; F is both draw 2 and blow 2 with the slide. The picker prefers slide-out, then the hole nearest the note you just played.

What the pencil left out

The handwritten numbers on the chart mark holes and arrows but not the slide, so G♯ and A share a “3”. Here G♯4 comes back as 3 blow, slide in — the accidentals stop being guesswork.

Built for solo-tuned chromatics (Hohner Chromonica / Super 64 family). Not for Richter-tuned diatonic harps, whose bends and layout work differently.
